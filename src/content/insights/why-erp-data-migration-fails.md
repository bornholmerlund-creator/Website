Data migration is the workstream most likely to sink an ERP go-live, and the one most consistently underestimated. Industry research repeatedly puts poor migration behind the majority of failures. The pattern in mid-sized businesses is remarkably consistent — and so is the fix.

## Why it goes wrong

**It starts too late.** Migration is treated as an IT task for the final phase: export from the old system, import to the new, hope it ties out. By then there's no time to discover that the legacy data is dirtier than anyone admitted.

**No one profiled the source.** Duplicate records, inconsistent units, orphaned references and abandoned fields accumulate over a decade. If you don't measure them up front, you meet them at 2am on cutover weekend.

**Mapping decisions aren't documented.** Under time pressure, someone decides how an old field maps to a new one — and that decision lives only in their head. When the numbers look wrong later, no one can retrace why.

**There's no reconciliation.** The data is loaded, but nothing proves it arrived intact. Record counts and financial balances are assumed, not verified.

## The four checks that prevent it

1. **Profile in week one.** Measure completeness, duplication and accuracy of the source data at the start of the programme, while there's still time to act.
2. **Document every mapping.** Treat transformation rules as auditable artefacts, not tribal knowledge.
3. **Rehearse with timed dry runs.** Run the full migration repeatedly before cutover until it's predictable. Cutover weekend should be the fifth time you've done it, not the first.
4. **Reconcile and prove it.** Deliver a pack that ties record counts and financial balances from source to target. Boring cutovers are reconciled cutovers.

Migration done this way stops being the risk that defines your project and becomes the part nobody worries about. That's the goal.
